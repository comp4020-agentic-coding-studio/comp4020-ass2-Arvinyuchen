## Course premise

Public guidance is the best summary we have of what helps people in general.
Sleep seven to nine hours ([Hirshkowitz et al.,
2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sleh.2014.12.010)). Aim for seven thousand
steps or more (a companion statement to the [24-Hour Movement
Guidelines](https://www.health.gov.au/topics/physical-activity/24-hour-movement-guidelines-for-all-australians/recommendations-for-adults-18-to-64-years)).
Enjoy a healthy breakfast (a practical tip in the [Australian Dietary
Guidelines](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/sites/default/files/files/the_guidelines/n55a_australian_dietary_guidelines_summary_book.pdf)
summary booklet, not one of the five guidelines themselves).
Notice that these are ranges and aims rather than instructions, and that none of
them knows anything about you. This course teaches you to close that gap in
three moves. Realisation: read the guidance accurately, then look at your own
day and find where you actually sit in it. Change: alter one thing, using the
guidance for what to change and how, which later lectures cover in detail.
Rethinking: read what comes back from your body, your mood and what you
actually did, and revise the change against it. Then go round again, because you
will not be the same person in May that you were in March. The unit is a single
day: twenty-four hours observed, adjusted, and observed again. What the course
will not do is hand you a finished answer, because a finished answer belongs to
one person across one stretch of days, and it is no substitute for a doctor.
What it gives you instead is the loop itself, and the judgement to know when a
result is worth acting on.

## Find the guidelines that suit you

Guidance is not one rule for everyone, and it has already been narrowed before
it reaches you. Three reasons the numbers are more specific than they look.

**Where you are.** The Australian sun rule is a single number: protect yourself
whenever the UV index reaches 3, and below 3 do not, so your skin can make
vitamin D. That one rule gives opposite instructions depending on where you
stand under it. In New South Wales the index is 3 or above for at least ten
months of the year. In the United Kingdom the shortfall runs the other way for
half of it, and everyone over four is advised to consider a ten microgram
vitamin D supplement from October to March, because there is not enough sun in
those months to make it.

If you assumed Australia's problem is the ozone hole, so did most people.
Cancer Council WA published an article titled "Why does Australia have so much
skin cancer? (Hint: it's not because of an ozone hole)", and the Australian
radiation agency's own modelling found the contribution of ozone depletion
small, and no larger here than in the United States despite far greater
depletion in the south. The duller reasons: Australians live closer to the
equator, the southern hemisphere passes about three per cent closer to the sun
during its summer, and the air is cleaner. Together, roughly fifteen per cent
more UV than the same latitude in the north. You have just watched the first
move of this course happen to you.

**When it is.** The same Australian rule switches off seasonally. In Hobart and
Melbourne the index sits below 3 through most of winter, when sun protection is
not recommended at all. Same country, same person, opposite instruction in July
and in January.

**Who you are.** The sleep guidance above already splits by age: seven to nine
hours for adults, seven to eight for older adults. The UK vitamin D advice
narrows again inside the country, recommending supplements all year rather than
only in winter for people who are rarely outdoors, who cover most of their
skin, or who have dark skin from Black African, Black Caribbean or South Asian
backgrounds, because melanin absorbs the UV that makes vitamin D.

That last category is where the narrowing runs out. It covers enormous
variation in skin, in where people live, in time spent outdoors. It is a better
guess than no guess, and it is still a guess about a group. Guidance narrows
from everyone to a group you belong to, and good guidance says so plainly. The
last step of this course is that same move, carried to the only group with no
variation left inside it.

We are in Australia, so start here.
