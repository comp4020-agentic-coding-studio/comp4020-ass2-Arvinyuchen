The most useful hour in the course, and the most uncomfortable.

We start with distributions rather than definitions: thirty days of one measure
on a screen, the worst day marked, and what happens next. The effect is visible
before any statistics are introduced, which is the point — this is not a subtle
artefact that appears under analysis. It is the shape of the data.

From there: Galton's 1886 measurements of parents and children, Kahneman's flight
instructors who built a training philosophy on it, and the reason the entire
self-improvement industry gets a permanent free lunch from the fact that people
start new routines on bad days.

We close on what it does to your own report, which is due next week. If you began
your experiment on an unusual day, some of your result belongs to the day.
