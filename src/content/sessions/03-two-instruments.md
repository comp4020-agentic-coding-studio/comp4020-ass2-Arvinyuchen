Tonight you measure one night twice.

A phone or watch on one side. A paper diary filled in at the moment you turn the
light off and the moment you get up, on the other. Same night, same person, two
numbers.

They will not match. That is not a malfunction, and the week is not about
deciding which one won.

## There is no referee in this room

In a sleep laboratory there is a third measure (electrodes, a technician, a
recording of your actual brain) and it settles the argument. You do not have
one. Neither do the millions of people making decisions about their lives based
on a number a wristband produced by guessing from wrist movement.

So you have two instruments and no arbiter. The honest report is not "I slept 6
hours 12 minutes". It is "one instrument said 6:12, the other said 7:05, and here
is which one I believed before I looked."

That last clause is the assessable part. Write it down first. It is remarkably
easy to discover, after seeing the numbers, that you always trusted the one that
agreed with you.

## The risk in this particular week

This is the first week with a real way to hurt yourself, and it is not sleep
deprivation. It is the measuring.

In 2017 a group of sleep clinicians gave a name to something they kept seeing:
patients arriving at clinic distressed about their sleep, whose sleep was fine,
and whose actual complaint was their tracker. They called it
[orthosomnia](/glossary/#orthosomnia): anxiety about sleeping correctly,
produced by the instrument meant to help. Some of those patients were sleeping
worse because of the data.

So: the stopping rule at the top of this page is not decoration. If you catch
yourself reaching for the number before you have got out of bed, stop. You have
found the effect this week is about, and you do not need to keep going to prove
it.

## In the lab

We put every pair of numbers on the board and look at the spread. Then we ask a
question that will run for the rest of the semester: given two instruments that
disagree by fifty minutes, what size of effect could you possibly detect next
week?

The answer is usually "a much bigger one than I was hoping to find".
