## What we do in the lab

A [baseline](/glossary/#baseline) is your day as it already is, before anyone
interferes with it. Every later week in this course compares something against
this one, so if you spend week 1 tidying, you spend the remaining eleven weeks
measuring against a day that never happened. There is no hypothesis this week,
nothing to prove and nothing to beat, and the only way to build it badly is to
make it interesting.

So we record twenty-four hours at whatever resolution you can actually sustain:
fifteen minutes is plenty, and an hour is not useless. Paper is fine. Your
phone is fine. What matters is that the record is continuous and that you wrote
it during the day rather than reconstructing it that night.

Then we do the part that feels like nothing: we read our own days back to each
other and notice what we left out. Everybody leaves something out. The
interesting question is not what you did with your Tuesday, it is which parts of
Tuesday you did not think were worth recording.

There is a sheet for all of it below, if a screen suits you better than paper.
It asks when you woke up and draws a line, and you pull that line down to open
a block: how far you pull it is how long the block lasted, a minute to the
pixel, and the space it opens is where you write what you were doing. At the
end of the day you press the sleep button, which records everything from there
back to waking as sleep and closes the twenty-four hours. Nothing has to be
dragged, and for most people typing is faster: every time on the sheet is a
field, and Enter walks from one block to the next. What it does not do is ask
you what you left out: that part is the hour in the lab, not a box on a
screen. It keeps nothing. It saves no copy, sends nothing anywhere, and loses
the lot when the tab closes, so print the sheet or save it as a PDF before you
leave the page.
