Do week 5 again. Same variable, same three phases, same measure, same length.

Then put the two results next to each other.

## The answer will have moved

For most of you the second result will differ from the first, sometimes
substantially, occasionally in the opposite direction. There is a strong instinct
about what this means: one of the two runs was done wrong.

Sometimes that is true, and looking for the mistake is the right first move. But
after you have looked and found nothing, there is a second explanation, and it is
the one this course has been building towards.

You are [non-stationary](/glossary/#non-stationary). The thing being measured
changed between March and May. Your sleep changed, your workload changed, the
daylight changed, and you have spent nine weeks learning to watch yourself, which
by week 4's logic has changed you too.

## Why this breaks the usual promise

Almost all advice about daily life assumes a stable subject. "Work in
twenty-five minute blocks" is a sentence about a person who stays the same. So is
every result you have produced this semester.

If the subject drifts, then a finding has a shelf life. Not because your method
was poor, but because the thing your method measured has moved on. This is why
the course cannot end with a list of what works for you: any specific answer
expires, and the only durable thing is the ability to run the measurement again.

## The long version

Santorio Santorio, in Venice, built himself a weighing chair and spent something
close to thirty years on it: weighing what he ate, what he excreted, and
himself, trying to account for the difference. He published in 1614.

Thirty years is the part to notice. He was not running an experiment; he was
maintaining an instrument, on a subject he knew would keep changing. That is much
closer to what this course is actually teaching than any single result in it.

## In the lab

Both numbers on the board, unaveraged. Then we ask everyone the same question:
which of your two answers would you act on, and what would you have to believe
about yourself to choose?
