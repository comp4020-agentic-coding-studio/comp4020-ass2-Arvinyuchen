Two thousand words, plus your record. You run the experiment you pre-registered
in week 6, you report what happened, and you say what the result is and is not
allowed to mean.

## What to hand in

Five parts, in this order. The word count covers the first four; the record is
not counted and is not a summary.

1. **The question**, copied from your pre-registration without editing it. If
   you want to report a different question, that is a departure, and it goes in
   part 2 with the others.
2. **What you did**, including every departure from the pre-registration, each
   with the date you made it and whether you had seen any data by then.
3. **What happened.** The numbers, and for each one, where in the record it came
   from.
4. **What you can conclude, and what you cannot.** This is the part the marking
   is mostly about, and it is where the sentence from the week 9 lab belongs:
   where the day your experiment began sat in your own record, and how much of
   your result you therefore cannot separate from the day.
5. **Your record**, whole and raw. The actual log, with the gaps left as gaps.

## The largest criterion is inference, not result

Forty-five per cent of this piece is whether your conclusion follows from your
data. Not whether your day improved. Not whether the intervention worked. Not
whether you found anything at all.

A report that says *"I measured this carefully for two weeks, and the honest
answer is that I cannot tell, and here is precisely why"* is a strong report. It
may be the strongest kind. A report that says *"this worked, I feel much
better"*, on a design that cannot support the claim, is a weak one no matter how
true the feeling is.

This is not a stylistic preference. **Marking the outcome would reward
[regression to the mean](/glossary/#regression-to-the-mean)**: most of you began
your experiments on an unremarkable or bad stretch, and most of you will have
improved regardless of what you did. A course that handed out marks for
improvement would be handing them out for arithmetic, and week 9 exists to show
you exactly that.

## What each criterion is looking for

Written out so that you can apply them to your own draft before a marker does,
and so that two markers reading the same report arrive at the same place.

### Whether your conclusion follows, 45 per cent

The marker reads your conclusion first and your design second, and asks whether
that design could have produced that sentence. Three rival explanations are
standing in the room for every one of you, and the report is expected to name
all three and either dispose of one or concede it: your own expectation, which
week 7 showed you cannot blind away; something else that moved at the same time,
which is week 8; and where your starting day sat in your own record, which is
week 9. A conclusion stated at a strength the design supports, with the three
named and handled, sits at the top of this criterion.

Marks come off for a claim the design cannot reach, for a conclusion that never
refers to a number in the record, and for leaving a rival unnamed. They do not
come off for a conclusion that is small, hedged, negative or uninteresting,
provided the hedging is doing real work rather than covering an absence of
thought. Nothing here is affected by whether the conclusion turns out to be
correct: nobody marking this knows, and your one subject cannot settle it.

### The limits you declare, 35 per cent

The test is not how many limits you list. It is whether each one changes what
the report is then allowed to say. A limit that appears in part 4 and leaves the
conclusion exactly where it was has not been used, and a report whose limits
section could be moved unchanged onto somebody else's experiment has not
declared any.

So: specific to this experiment, quantified wherever your record can quantify it,
and visibly narrowing the conclusion. "This study has limitations" earns nothing.
"I lost the Thursday and the Friday of the removal week, which leaves three days
in that phase against seven in the others, so the comparison is between a week
and a long weekend" earns a lot.

Departures are marked here too. Deviating from your pre-registration is normal
and costs you nothing. An undeclared departure that the marker finds by reading
your record against your pre-registration costs you this criterion, and it is
the one thing in this course that reads as dishonest rather than inexperienced.

### Whether a stranger could re-run this, 20 per cent

Literal, and checked literally. The marker takes your record and asks whether
they could produce your numbers from it without asking you a question. That
needs the measure and its units written down, the time of day it was taken, and
every number in the report traceable to a dated line. Missing days appear as
gaps, because a record with the gaps quietly closed up is a record that cannot
be checked.

Presentation is not marked. Neat handwriting, charts and a title page are worth
nothing here, and a photograph of a legible paper log loses nothing.

## Null results

If nothing happened, report that nothing happened, at the same length and with
the same care you would have given a positive result.

A null result is not the absence of a finding, and the difference is where the
marks are. "I found no difference" on its own says nothing, because a design
that could not have detected a difference produces that sentence too. The
version that scores is "I found no difference, and given how far my two
instruments disagreed with each other in week 3, the smallest difference I could
have detected was larger than the one I went looking for."

The reason this is insisted on is not politeness about disappointing semesters.
[Week 12](/sessions/12-unmeasured/) carries the numbers on what happened to a
literature that published only the trials that found something, and the personal
version has the same shape. A semester of reports that all found something would
tell us only that we had taught you to find things.

## If the stopping rule fired

Stopping early is a result and is reported as one. Say when it fired, what it
fired on, and what the days you did collect can and cannot support, which will
usually be less than you hoped and more than nothing. A report built on four
days that is honest about being built on four days can score above a complete
one that is not.
