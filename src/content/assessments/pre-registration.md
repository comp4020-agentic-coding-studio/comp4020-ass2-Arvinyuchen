Two pages, handed in before you start. That ordering is the entire point, and it
is the only deadline in this course that cannot be extended after the fact — a
pre-registration submitted once you have seen your data is not a
pre-registration, whatever it says on it.

## What goes in it

1. **The question**, in one sentence. Not "does the Pomodoro technique work", but
   "does a twenty-five minute block beat a forty minute block for me, on this
   measure, this fortnight".
2. **The measure**, operationalised as in week 2. Say how you will record it and
   how long each recording takes.
3. **The design**, including how you are balancing the order of your conditions.
4. **The stopping rule.** When you will stop early, and what would make you.
5. **What would prove you wrong.** A specific result — a number, a direction, a
   pattern — that you would accept as the timer losing.

## Why the fifth one carries the most marks

Because it is the one that cannot be written afterwards.

Everything else in the list can be reconstructed from memory once you know how
things turned out, and reconstructed honestly, with no intent to mislead. What
cannot be reconstructed is what you would have counted as failure before you
knew. Writing it down in advance is the only way that sentence means anything.

## A note on what is being marked

Your expectation does not have to be correct. You are not marked on whether the
result came out the way you predicted, and a pre-registration whose prediction
was comprehensively wrong can score full marks. You are marked on whether the
document was specific enough to be wrong at all.
